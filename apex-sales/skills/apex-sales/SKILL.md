# Apex Sales

## When To Use This
Anything sales-facing for Misael Systems and Apex Zero: call prep, outreach, objection replies, proposals, and reviewing marketing (ads, reels, captions, the ZERO videos) for claims that break the rules below.

## Who Sells What
- **Misael Systems** is the company. Reps introduce themselves as calling from Misael Systems.
- **Apex Zero** is the software it offers: it helps home-service businesses stop missing calls while they're on the job site or after hours. It is the product behind system 1 below. In marketing, Apex Zero's hero character is **ZERO**.
- Beyond Apex Zero, Misael Systems builds connected infrastructure for home-service businesses: the systems behind leads, estimates, follow-up, reviews and operations. Sell a fix for one specific operational problem, not "AI." Every build is scoped, designed, built, tested and handed off, and works with the client's existing tools when technically feasible.

## The Six Systems (pain → build → question to ask)
1. Speed-to-lead and missed-call recovery (Apex Zero). Pain: unanswered calls on the job site or after hours, slow replies, leads go to a competitor. Build: configured text-back or response for supported missed calls and lead sources; may qualify or route. Ask: "What happens when you miss a call? How fast does someone follow up?"
2. Quote and estimate follow-up. Pain: estimates sent, nobody follows up. Build: SMS/email sequences on supported estimate events. Ask: "Who follows up on open estimates? How many are open?"
3. CRM and field software integration. Pain: double entry, systems that don't talk. Build: connect supported tools (ServiceTitan, Jobber, Housecall Pro, QuickBooks, Salesforce, HubSpot), subject to access and feasibility. Ask: "What software do you use? Where does your team enter things twice?"
4. Automated Google review requests. Pain: reviews depend on someone remembering. Build: review request after a supported job-completion event. Ask: "How do you ask for reviews now?"
5. Database reactivation. Pain: past customers never contacted for repeat or seasonal work. Build: reactivation for eligible past customers, tune-ups, maintenance plans. Ask: "Do you contact past customers? How do you know who's due?"
6. Custom integrations and workflows. Pain: workarounds where software falls short. Build: custom connections and internal workflows. Ask: "What still needs a workaround?"

## The Method
1. Qualify. Best fit: established HVAC, plumbing, roofing, electrical, pest control, landscaping, garage door, cleaning and similar field-service companies with steady inquiries, estimates and jobs, a real process problem, and a reason fixing it matters.
2. Discover before pitching. Ask:
   - Walk me through what happens from a new inquiry to the job being booked.
   - What happens when nobody answers the phone?
   - Who follows up when an estimate hasn't been approved?
   - Where does your team enter the same information more than once?
   - What happens after a job is marked complete?
   - How do you contact past customers who may need another service?
   - If you could fix one operational headache, which would matter most?
   Listen for: a repeated problem, how often, who handles it, what it costs, and whether they want it fixed.
3. Recommend only the system that fits what you heard. Never assume they need all six.
4. Explain it in plain English. Example flow: lead comes in → response triggers → info syncs to CRM → estimate triggers follow-up → completed job triggers review request → past customers enter reactivation. Say it is an example, not what every client gets.
5. Next step is a short assessment call, then the four stages: Discover → Identify → Design & Build → Test & Hand Off.

## Cold Call
- Opening: "Hey [Name], it's [Rep] over at Misael Systems. I know I'm calling out of the blue, but can I take about 30 seconds to explain why I called, and then you can tell me if it's relevant?"
- Pitch: "We help home-service businesses connect the systems behind their operations, like missed-call follow-up, estimate nurturing, CRM integrations and customer reactivation, so less of the process depends on someone doing everything manually."
- Discovery: "Out of curiosity, how are you currently handling missed calls and following up on estimates?"
- Close: "It sounds like there may be something worth looking at. The next step is to understand your current setup and see whether there's a practical solution. Would you be open to a short assessment call?"

## Objections
- "We already have software." → We're not necessarily replacing it; we look at whether your tools work together and whether your team still moves information by hand.
- "We already handle that." → Is it working consistently, or are there still gaps your team works around?
- "Send me information." → Sure. Which is most relevant: missed-call follow-up, estimate nurturing, or connecting your existing software?
- "How much?" → It depends on the systems involved and what needs building; we understand the setup and scope before a defined proposal.
- "Can you guarantee revenue?" → We don't guarantee revenue. We define the system and test the agreed workflows; results depend on several factors.
- "Can you integrate with our software?" → We confirm the platform, access and requirements before promising feasibility.

## Rules (never break these, in calls or marketing)
- Never guarantee revenue, leads, conversions or ROI.
- Never imply every missed call becomes a customer or every estimate converts.
- Never promise a platform can be integrated before technical review.
- No final scope or delivery dates before requirements are understood.
- Don't assume a client needs all six systems.
- Don't promise unlimited changes or support unless included.
- Slow down or walk away when: no clear problem or no interest in fixing it, they want guaranteed results, or they want compatibility promised before access is checked.

## Mistakes To Avoid
- Pitching AI instead of the problem.
- Pitching before discovery.
- Quoting price or timelines on the first call.
